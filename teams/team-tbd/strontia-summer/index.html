For grown-ups: where this comes from
  • The snow on the mountains follows the Hoosier Pass snowpack record; the mountains themselves are a drawing, not the real skyline. Strontia Springs Reservoir is on the South Platte River above Denver. The sensor is a profiling sonde that Denver Water lowers through the water several times a day. Every reading here is from its export for 7 April to 19 August 2026, one profile per day, with river flow, temperature and turbidity from the USGS gage and Colorado DWR upstream, rain from NOAA station USC00058022, and snowpack from the Hoosier Pass SNOTEL station.
  • Dots map to measurements: plants are chlorophyll, mud is turbidity, bubbles are dissolved oxygen, flecks are specific conductance compensated to 25 C. How many dots appear, and how deep they sit, follows that day's profile. The water's shade of blue follows temperature. Depth is the sonde's vertical position as recorded, unit unstated.
  • River water sinks to the first depth where the reservoir is as cold as the river, which is how a plunging inflow behaves. The water level follows Denver Water's daily storage record for Strontia Springs from the Colorado DWR, with its rise and fall exaggerated many times over so it can be seen. The fish and the splashes are for fun. Fish swim where oxygen was highest that day.
  • Built by build.py in this folder. The technical version of this view is strontia-section.html at the repository root.
