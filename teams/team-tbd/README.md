# Team TBD: inside Strontia Springs Reservoir during a storm

Scenario 2 of the Design Storm: *how do water quality parameters change and distribute by
depth in the reservoir, and how do storms and spring runoff change that picture?*

We turned Denver Water's profiling sonde record (Apr 7 to Aug 19, 2026) into a
depth-by-time picture of the reservoir, followed the August 14 to 15 storm from the river
gage through the reservoir to the Foothills plant, and put the result on the Strontia
Springs Dam marker of a copy of the 3D map. The map copy and the pop-out page also fold in
another team member's Foothills TOC/alkalinity prediction dashboard (`dashboard/`), as a
map overlay button and as a Dashboard tab.

**Readings are provisional** and the data terms below apply to everything here.

## The short version

The storm's muddy water entered the reservoir as a **layer about 8 to 18 m deep**, not at
the surface or the bottom. It was strongest at 13 m about a day and a half after the gage
peak, then sank to about 20 m and was still there when the record ends four days later. The
plant's organic carbon rose from 2.0 to 2.5 mg/L the day after the gage peak. Whether the
plant was drawing from the layer depends on the intake depth, which we do not know. Full
findings with the evidence behind each one: [`FINDINGS.md`](FINDINGS.md).

Along the way:
- The sonde's `Conductivity` is **not temperature-compensated**. Corrected to 25 °C it
  matches the river gage and is flat with depth; the raw column's layering is temperature.
- **Sensor servicing** shows up as simultaneous steps at every depth (seven dates). ORP and
  chlorophyll cannot be compared across them.
- Readings deeper than 45 m in April and early May look like the sonde **touching sediment**.

All of this is in [`analysis/QC.md`](analysis/QC.md), generated by the cleaning script.

(`http://localhost:8765/teams/team-tbd/map/strontia.html`), the same data in its own page, for

saturation), turbidity and specific conductance on one time axis with a linked crosshair, for
the storm window or the whole season. Every number in the lessons comes from
`analysis/build_lessons.py`; its chat answers at a 13 to 16 reading level.

## Regenerating everything

Needs Python 3.11+ with a venv in this folder (the system Python has no pandas):

```
cd teams/team-tbd
uv venv .venv && uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python pandas numpy openpyxl scipy matplotlib scikit-learn
# or: python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install pandas numpy openpyxl scipy matplotlib scikit-learn

.venv/bin/python analysis/clean_sonde.py          # -> analysis/out/sonde_clean.csv, casts.csv, analysis/QC.md
.venv/bin/python analysis/grid_sonde.py           # -> analysis/out/grid.npz, heat_*.png
.venv/bin/python analysis/storm_trace.py          # -> analysis/out/storm_numbers.json, storm_casts.csv, storm_timeline.png
.venv/bin/python autoresearch/prepare.py          # -> autoresearch/data/ (fixed held-out blocks, seeded)
.venv/bin/python analysis/fill_best.py            # -> analysis/out/grid_best.npz, compare_*.png (Phase 5 winner)
.venv/bin/python analysis/build_profile_json.py   # -> map/strontia-profile.json
.venv/bin/python analysis/build_lessons.py        # -> map/strontia-lessons.json (prints each number and its source)
```

Each step reads only the originals in `data/` and `strontia-brief/` plus the previous step's
output; nothing needs the network. `analysis/out/` is gitignored (about 3 MB, regenerable).

To score the fill-in method: `.venv/bin/python autoresearch/evaluate.py` (dev; add
`--detail` for each parameter). `--lockbox` is for the final check only.

## Fill-in experiment (Phase 5)

Question: how best to fill in the reservoir between casts? The harness
(`autoresearch/prepare.py`, `evaluate.py`, locked) hides 99 contiguous 6-hour blocks, usually
one whole cast each, and splits them into **dev** (the loop sees its score) and a **lockbox**
(scored once, at the end). The score is RMSE on the hidden readings divided by each
parameter's standard deviation and averaged over temperature, conductivity, log turbidity,
dissolved oxygen and chlorophyll. Lower is better. One agent ran 15 experiments; every one is
in [`autoresearch/results.tsv`](autoresearch/results.tsv).

| method | dev | lockbox |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 2 linear (baseline) | 0.26904 | 0.18987 |
| best (experiment 15) | 0.26098 (−3.0%) | 0.17887 (−5.8%) |

The best method also improves every parameter on the lockbox. It removes a daily warming
cycle, fitted per 1 m depth band, before interpolating in time between casts, then adds the
cycle back. It also applies a light running median down each cast to turbidity and
chlorophyll, and keeps temperature within the two neighbouring casts. A slightly better
version on dev (0.26031) was rejected because it put about 19% of temperature values
outside the neighbouring casts, which is not physical.

It won on the lockbox and its picture looks sensible (`analysis/out/compare_*.png`:
temperature within about 0.1 °C of the linear fill, with the storm layer's shape unchanged),
so the map panel uses it. The gain is modest, and the storm story is the same with either method.
No step improved by more than 30%, so the leakage check was never triggered.
The agent never read the lockbox; it was scored once, by the main session, after the loop
stopped.

## Questions for Denver Water

1. **Where is the Foothills intake, in depth or elevation, in Strontia Springs?** This decides
   whether the plant drew from the storm layer (about 8 to 18 m).
2. **Is `Vertical Position` depth below the surface?** The temperature evidence says yes.
   Where is the sonde relative to the dam and the intake?
3. **Is the sonde's `Conductivity` temperature-compensated?** The data says it is not; after
   correcting to 25 °C it matches the USGS gage.
4. **What happened on the servicing dates** (Apr 28, May 4, May 14, May 18, Jun 16, Jul 20,
   Aug 18)? Cleaning, recalibration, redeployment? Is there a service log?
5. Did the sonde touch bottom in April and early May (readings deeper than 45 m)? Why did the
   profiler's starting depth change to about 44 m in mid-May?
6. From Jul 13, about one cast in five stops in the top 5 m. Intentional?
7. **What time are the Foothills TOC and alkalinity samples taken, and what time zone are the
   sonde timestamps in?** Needed to turn "the day after" into hours.
8. What do you already do with the sonde data, so we build on it rather than repeat it?
9. How soon after sampling are Foothills lab results available? (Relevant to the persistence
   baseline finding in `PLAN.md`: "today = the lab value from 2 days ago" beat the random
   forest in every test season.)
10. How far back do Foothills TOC and alkalinity lab records go?
11. What were the units of `Phycocyanin`?
